DESIGN STUDIO

Procurement Tool

Project management tool for procurements that facilitates the procurement process. The purpose is to provide an effect of reduced workload for staff.

CLIENT

Dizparc

DURATION

7 weeks

MY ROLE

Researcher, UX/UI designer

METHODS

Workshop, mapping requirements, analysises, sketches, personas, wireframes, prototype.

GOAL

Minimize stress for people working with procurements and improve the project structure.

Background & Motivation

Client

This project was carried out in collaboration with Dizparc, who acted as the client and provided the project brief, requirements, and industry insights.

Problem Statement

Procurement processes are often complex, time-consuming, and difficult to manage. Poor communication and scattered documentation can lead to inefficiencies, stress and extra work.

Assigned requirements

In this project, the client assigned a lot of design requirements that needed to be addressed in the solution. These requirements guided the design process and helped ensure the final product met the client's needs.

Procurement processes are often complex, time-consuming, and difficult to navigate, creating unnecessary stress and inefficiencies for employees. This project explored how a procurement tool could be designed with a strong focus on user experience (UX) and user interface (UI) design, with the goal of creating a more intuitive and user-friendly procurement experience.

What’s procurement?

Procurement is a process in which organizations, often public authorities, purchase goods or services by announcing their needs and inviting suppliers to submit offers. The process is used to evaluate these offers and determine which supplier will be awarded the contract, with the goal of ensuring a transparent and fair outcome.

Procurement process

The procurement process varies widely, making it challenging to define a general process. Our goal was to understand what works well and what does not in order to identify areas for improvement. We based our work on a general procurement model from the , supported by insights from a workshop conducted during the project.

My Contribution

My main contribution was transforming research and design requirements into a practical design solution. I contributed throughout the design process, from concept development and prototyping to refining the solution through feedback and iteration, ensuring the final concept was intuitive, structured, and user-centered.

Design Process

Discovery & Research

Discovery

The discovery was conducted through preparatory work to gain a deeper understanding of the problem area, users, and project requirements. This phase provided a solid foundation for informed design decisions throughout the project.

Research

Research was condected to gain insights into users, their needs, and the challenges within the procurement process. These insights helped guide the design process, design decisions and shape the final solution.

1. Domain knowledge

To gain a deeper understanding of procurements, knowledge was conducted into existing processes, challenges, and stakeholder needs. This knowledge provided a foundation for creating a relevant and effective design solution.

2. Mapping requirements

The design requirements were mapped to create a clear understanding of the project's goals, user needs, and key challenges. This provided a structured foundation for the subsequent design process.

3. Workshop

A workshop was conducted to identify additional user needs that could be translated into design requirements for the procurement tool. The workshop provided insights into what a procurement process typically looks like and what aspects need improvement → user needs.

4. Personas

Personas were created based on insights from the workshop. Since the procurement tool is intended to support three different roles, this was done to identify similarities and differences between the roles. But also to develop a clearer picture of potential users and their needs.

Idea generation

The idea generation phase focused on exploring and visualizing possible solutions. Brainwriting and sketching were used to generate concepts and identify designs that best addressed user needs and project requirements.

Brainwriting 6-3-5

To generate proposals for how the procurement tool could be designed, we used a brainstorming method called Brainwriting 6-3-5. This method resulted in several sketches illustrating potential designs for the tool.

Sketches

A number of sketches were created with the aim of including all necessary features and visualizing how they could appear in the procurement tool. The goal was to ensure that no design requirements were excluded.

Visualizations

Visualizations were used to map the procurement process and understand how users experience different stages. Service blueprints helped illustrate both user-facing interactions and the supporting processes required behind the scenes.

Customer journey map

A customer journey map was created to visualize the procurement process from the users' perspective. By mapping user actions, emotions, and interactions throughout the process, key pain points and specific areas for improvement were identified. This provided valuable insights that helped guide design decisions.

Service blueprint

A service blueprint was created to visualize both user-facing interactions and the supporting processes behind the scenes. This helped identify how different parts of the service were connected and provided insights that informed design decisions.

Prototyping

Ideas were translated into digital prototypes through low-fidelity wireframes and a high-fidelity prototype. This phase focused on refining both the user experience and visual design of the procurement tool.

Low-fidelity wireframes

To develop the different ideas, digital low-fidelity wireframes were created. This was done with the aim of focusing on UX and information architecture, ensuring that the tool would be easy to navigate and intuitive for users.

High-fidelity prototype

Based on our wireframes, a high-fidelity funcional prototype was developed. During this phase, the focus was UI design, where the aim was to convey a calm impression through colors, while maintaining a professional appearance.

Result & Key features

Project overview

Design motivation

This feature gives users a overview of the process, making it easier to understand tasks and deadlines. By displaying activities in a timeline and providing information through a notice board, the feature aims to improve awareness, planning, and timely completion of documentation.

Addresses the design requirements

  • Structure projects and tasks
  • Follow the procurement process
  • Enable communication among parties

Document management

Design motivation

Within the project overview, there is a section for document management where users can see documents, the responsible person, deadlines, and upload documents. The aim is to compile all documentation so nothing gets lost. The ability to comment on document provides communication.

Addresses the design requirements

  • Document management
  • Create and assign tasks
  • Structure projects and responsibilities

Meeting management

Design motivation

This feature gives users a overview of all meetings related to the procurement. The goal is to ensure that every project member can see what will be discussed, see which documents need to be ready, and communicate with other attendees to clarify questions. Past meetings are included.

Addresses the design requirements

  • Meeting management
  • Communication between parties
  • Structure projects and responsibilities

Staff management

Design motivation

This feature provides users with a clear overview of staff and project members. The purpose is to make it easier to communicate with others. This helps clarify questions about documents. Instead of redoing work due to a lack of communication, previously completed work is preserved.

Addresses the design requirements

  • Access management
  • Management of staff and members
  • Communication between parties

Archive

Design motivation

This feature allows users to review documents from past procurements. The purpose is to reduce the amount of work required in future procurements and to ensure that no work gets lost. If documents and resources from earlier procurements is available, the same expert knowledge can be reused.

Addresses the design requirements

  • Document from previous processes
  • Document management

My page

Design motivation

A personal page was included to give every user an individual overview of all tasks assigned to them. This is helpful if, for example, a procurement officer is involved in multiple procurements. The feature allows them to see all activities across different projects in one place, making it easier to prioritize tasks.

Addresses the design requirements

  • Ability to structure projects and tasks
  • Management of staff and members

Potential effects

By addressing identified pain points in the procurement process, the proposed design could improve usability, reduce complexity, and support more efficient collaboration between stakeholders. The solution also has the potential to increase awareness of procurement activities and help users complete tasks on time.
